CMSC471 · Interactive explanation

The Sound of Spotify, 2015–2025

Authors: Jiho Lee, John Li, Ed Yun, Andrew Zhang

The story: popular Spotify tracks do not move in one clean direction. Mood, energy, danceability, and duration shift differently by era and segment, so each chart narrows the question from broad trend to specific profile.

What this page is trying to answer

This project asks whether popular Spotify tracks from 2015-2025 share a recognizable sound profile, and whether that profile changes once you look by year range or segment. The goal is not to find a single formula for popularity. Instead, the page treats popularity as the outcome of several musical signals moving together: mood, energy, danceability, tempo, duration, and era.

The page starts with the full set of high-performing tracks, then lets you narrow the lens. Every chart after the filter uses the same selected tracks, so the sections build one argument rather than separate observations: first the broad trend, then the selected profile, then the era context, then a set of simple claims that either hold up or break down.

Start broad. Look for the overall movement in mood, energy, and danceability.

Narrow the lens. Use years or segment to test whether the pattern changes.

Pressure test it. Check whether simple explanations like tempo or duration are enough.

End with a profile. The capstone summarizes what stands out for the current filter.

What the features mean

Valence is Spotify's estimate of musical positivity, used here as mood.

Energy captures intensity and activity, not whether a song is happy.

Danceability describes how steady and movement-friendly the track feels.

Popularity is Spotify's 0-100 platform index, not raw streams.
